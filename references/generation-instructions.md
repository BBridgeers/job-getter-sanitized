# AI GENERATOR INSTRUCTIONS FOR JOB OPPORTUNITY ACQUISITION GUIDES

## COMPREHENSIVE QUALITY STANDARDS & GENERATION RULES

---

## DOCUMENT PURPOSE

This document provides **explicit instructions** for AI systems generating customized job opportunity acquisition guides from the generic template. These instructions ensure consistent quality, depth, and professionalism across all generated guides.

---

## CORE GENERATION PRINCIPLES

### 1. DEPTH OVER BREVITY

**Rule:** Always err on the side of comprehensive detail rather than brevity.

**Examples:**
- ❌ "Experience with customer success" 
- ✅ "14+ years B2B SaaS and enterprise consultative sales/customer success, regularly defining, executing, and iterating client success programs and operating rhythms for Enterprise/MM clients with lifecycle ownership and recurring QBR/SBR sessions"

**Minimum Word Counts by Section:**
- Section 2 (Match Analysis): 500+ words
- Section 4 (Product Overview): 400+ words
- Section 5 (Company Overview): 300+ words
- Section 8 (STAR Stories): 150-250 words EACH (10 stories required)
- Section 10 (30-60-90 Plan): 200+ words PER phase

### 2. SPECIFICITY & QUANTIFICATION

**Rule:** Every achievement, capability, or claim must include specific numbers, percentages, or measurable outcomes.

**Examples:**
- ❌ "Improved customer retention"
- ✅ "Drove 95%+ customer retention rate across enterprise SaaS portfolio by implementing proactive health-scoring model that identified at-risk accounts 45 days earlier than industry standard"

**Required Quantification:**
- Percentages (retention rates, improvement metrics, match scores)
- Dollar amounts (ARR recovered, expansion revenue, deal sizes)
- Time periods (days to value, timeline reductions)
- Portfolio sizes (number of accounts, users, transactions)
- Volume metrics (QBRs conducted, implementations completed)

### 3. PROFESSIONAL YET PERSONALIZED TONE

**Rule:** Maintain executive-level professionalism while avoiding robotic or overly formal language.

**Characteristics:**
- ✅ Confident without arrogance
- ✅ Data-driven without being dry
- ✅ Strategic without buzzwords
- ✅ Natural transitions and varied sentence structure
- ❌ Marketing fluff or hyperbole
- ❌ Overuse of superlatives ("exceptional," "outstanding," "superior")
- ❌ Robotic parallelism or repetitive phrasing

**Tone Examples:**
- Opening: "I'm writing to express my strong interest..." (natural)
- Not: "I am pleased to submit my application for consideration..." (stiff)
- Achievement: "Throughout my career, I've built my reputation on..." (personal)
- Not: "Consistently achieved results through..." (robotic)

### 4. CONTEXTUAL RELEVANCE

**Rule:** Every section must connect user's background to the specific job requirements. No generic content.

**Implementation:**
- Reference specific company products/platforms by name
- Connect user achievements to company's stated challenges
- Use industry-specific terminology from the job posting
- Demonstrate understanding of company's competitive position
- Show awareness of recent company news/developments

### 5. ACTIONABLE & TACTICAL

**Rule:** Frameworks and strategies must be specific enough to execute immediately, not high-level theory.

**Examples:**
- ✅ "Conduct root-cause analysis using 5 Whys methodology, deploy intensive re-onboarding program with daily check-ins for 14 days, facilitate executive sponsor alignment via executive steering committee"
- ❌ "Use best practices to address customer issues and improve relationships"

## SECTION-BY-SECTION GENERATION STANDARDS

### SECTION 1: Job Description (Verbatim)

**Requirements:**
- Copy job description EXACTLY as posted (no paraphrasing)
- Preserve original bullet formatting
- Include all listed responsibilities, requirements, and competencies
- If job description is sparse, supplement with research but mark additions

### SECTION 2: Match/Quality Analysis

**Structure:** 5 subsections (A-E) + Quality percentages + Proof points

**Subsection Requirements:**
- **A. Strategic Vision:** 3-4 bullets on program ownership, enterprise scale, QBR/SBR cadence
- **B. Relationship Management:** 3-4 bullets on multi-threaded portfolios, C-suite engagement, communication artifacts
- **C. Cross-Functional Orchestration:** 3-4 bullets on PMO/Product/Support coordination, RACI, migration experience
- **D. Adoption & Expansion:** 3-4 bullets on health scoring, MEDDIC, churn prevention, expansion methodology
- **E. Solution Development:** 3-4 bullets on playbook authorship, ROI quantification, business case prep

**Quality Percentages:**
- Calculate match score for 8-10 capability areas
- Use semantic similarity + keyword overlap
- Present as: "Strategic vision, business review execution: **95%**"
- Include justification for each score

**Proof Points:**
- 4-6 quantified achievements
- Each includes: metric + context + outcome
- Directly maps to job requirements
- Example: "Built and tracked health portfolios—moved 20-30% from amber/red to green via focused adoption and exec alignment"

**Total Word Count:** 500+ words

### SECTION 3: Skills Gap Analysis

**Structure:** Weaknesses + Mitigation strategies

**Weakness Format:**
```
**[Gap Name]:** If most prior work is [user background] with some [target industry]-adjacent exposure, you may be comparatively "lighter" on direct [specific domain] workflows. Sell around by framing as "[differentiator angle]," [supporting points].
```

**Requirements:**
- Identify 2-4 realistic gaps (don't fabricate if no gaps exist)
- Each gap includes: honest assessment + reframe strategy + specific talking points
- Mitigation section has 4-5 bullets converting gaps to differentiators

### SECTION 4: Product Overview

**5W Framework:** WHO, WHAT, WHEN/WHERE, WHY

**WHO:** 
- Target user personas (50+ words)
- Include: departments, roles, operational teams, external stakeholders

**WHAT:** 
- Detailed product description (100+ words)
- Platform type, core modules, key capabilities
- Scale metrics (users, transactions, countries, spend managed)
- Example: "Cloud-based CMMS serving 7M+ users across 140 countries, processing 19M work orders annually"

**WHEN/WHERE:**
- Use cases and industries (50+ words)
- Specific scenarios where product excels

**WHY (Strategic Value):**
- 8-12 bullet points covering:
  - Operational transformation
  - Cost reduction (with percentages)
  - Workflow automation (with metrics)
  - ROI data (cite studies)
  - Time-to-value and payback periods
  - Multi-year benefit projections

**Key Features:**
- 8-15 bullet list of core features/modules

**Selling Point:**
- 2-3 sentences on competitive advantage
- Reference specific customer segments

**Total Word Count:** 400+ words

### SECTION 5: Company Overview

**Required Components:**
- Company snapshot (name, HQ, industry, size, founded, website, stage)
- Mission & values (100+ words, quoted if available)
- Strategic positioning analysis (150+ words)
- Recent news (3-5 developments with dates)
- Career value proposition (100+ words connecting to user's goals)

**Total Word Count:** 300+ words

### SECTION 8: STAR Stories

**Requirements:**
- Generate 10 complete stories
- Each story: 150-250 words
- Follow strict STAR format:
  - **Situation:** Context, challenge, stakes (2-3 sentences)
  - **Task:** Specific responsibility, goal, constraints (1-2 sentences)
  - **Action:** Detailed steps taken (4-6 bullet points or 3-4 sentences)
  - **Result:** Quantified outcomes, impact, recognition (2-3 sentences with numbers)

**Story Topics (required):**
1. Rescued high-value at-risk account
2. Drove expansion/upsell success
3. Led complex technical implementation
4. Built health scoring/risk model
5. Improved customer satisfaction/NPS
6. Cross-functional collaboration
7. Reduced time-to-value
8. Strategic executive business review
9. Change management/adoption
10. Data-driven decision making

**Quality Standards:**
- Each story must include: specific customer/situation, quantified challenge, detailed actions, measurable results
- Results must include percentages, dollar amounts, or other metrics
- Stories must be believable and specific (no generic examples)

### SECTION 10: 30-60-90 Day Plan

**Structure:** 3 phases, each with Objectives + Activities + Deliverables + Metrics

**Days 1-30: LEARN & ASSESS**
- Objectives: 2-3 primary goals (portfolio assessment, stakeholder mapping, product mastery)
- Activities: 8-12 specific activities (account reviews, intro meetings, training, shadowing)
- Deliverables: 4-6 tangible outputs (health report, relationship map, strategy presentation)
- Metrics: 3-5 measurable success criteria

**Days 31-60: EXECUTE & OPTIMIZE**
- Objectives: 2-3 execution goals (quick wins, QBR execution, adoption initiatives)
- Activities: 8-12 activities (customer engagement, process implementation, cross-functional collab)
- Deliverables: 4-6 outputs (QBR completions, adoption gains, process improvements)
- Metrics: 3-5 measurable outcomes

**Days 61-90: SCALE & INNOVATE**
- Objectives: 2-3 innovation goals (advocacy programs, scalable processes, strategic recommendations)
- Activities: 8-12 activities (expansion pipeline, playbook creation, team enablement)
- Deliverables: 4-6 strategic outputs (expansion pipeline, improved playbooks, strategic recommendations)
- Metrics: 3-5 quantified results (portfolio health improvement, expansion $ generated, NPS increase)

**Total Word Count:** 200+ words per phase (600+ total)

### SECTION 41: Cover Letter

**Structure:** Opening + Achievement Paragraph + Company Fit + Readiness + Location + Closing

**Requirements:**
- 350-450 words total
- 5-6 paragraphs
- Professional yet personable tone
- Include specific company/product references
- Include 2-3 quantified achievements
- Express genuine enthusiasm
- Reference location advantage if applicable

**Opening Paragraph:**
- Express strong interest in [specific role] at [specific company]
- Mention years of experience and core value proposition
- Hook with confidence statement

**Achievement Paragraph:**
- Highlight 2-3 key achievements with specific numbers
- Connect achievements to role requirements
- Use phrases like "Throughout my career, I've built my reputation on..."

**Company Fit Paragraph:**
- Demonstrate company research (product, mission, recent developments)
- Explain why this opportunity excites you personally
- Reference specific capabilities or metrics (e.g., "238% ROI")

**Readiness Paragraph:**
- Emphasize immediate contribution potential
- Mention specific relevant skills/experience
- Show strategic mindset + tactical execution balance

**Location Paragraph (if applicable):**
- Mention location advantage (local, no relocation, community knowledge)
- Express immediate availability

**Closing:**
- Invite conversation
- Express gratitude
- Professional sign-off

### SECTION 42-43: Resume Enhancement

**Executive Summary Requirements:**
- 75-100 words
- 3-4 sentences
- Include: years of experience, 3-4 core competencies, 2-3 signature achievements with numbers, unique value proposition

**Core Competencies:**
- 18-24 competencies in 3-line format, pipe-separated
- Mix: hard skills (40%), soft skills (30%), tools (30%)
- Must include all job posting keywords

**Resume Bullets:**
- 8-12 bullets per role (most recent), decreasing for older roles
- Each bullet: Action verb + Context + Specific action + Quantified result
- Examples:
  - "Drove 95%+ customer retention rate across enterprise SaaS portfolio by implementing proactive health-scoring model that identified at-risk accounts 45 days earlier than industry standard, enabling targeted intervention strategies"
  - "Recovered $8.2M in threatened ARR through systematic churn prevention playbook, conducting root-cause analysis, deploying intensive re-onboarding programs, and facilitating executive sponsor alignment across 12 at-risk enterprise accounts"

**Achievements Callout:**
- 6-10 highest-impact metrics in arrow format
- Example: "→ 95%+ Customer Retention Rate (Enterprise Segment)"

## VARIABLE POPULATION RULES

### Data Source Priority

When populating {{VARIABLES}}, use this priority order:

1. **Job-specific data** from jobs database (Fields 1-43)
2. **Enrichment data** from Stage 2 research
3. **User profile data** from user_profile table
4. **Inferred data** from semantic analysis of job posting
5. **Industry standards** if no specific data available

### Handling Missing Data

**If variable has no data:**
- Section 1-10 (core sections): Use "[Research Needed]" placeholder if critical, or omit variable if optional
- Section 11-43: Infer from context or use industry-standard assumptions
- Document what was inferred in generation log

**Never:**
- Leave {{VARIABLE}} unpopulated in final output
- Use obviously fake data (e.g., "Company XYZ founded in YYYY")
- Generate completely fabricated company history or metrics

### Variable Formatting

**Company/Product Names:**
- Use official capitalization from website
- Include proper formatting (e.g., "JLL Technologies" not "jll technologies")

**Metrics:**
- Use appropriate precision (95%+, not 94.7%)
- Include currency symbols ($8.2M, not 8.2M dollars)
- Use appropriate abbreviations (K, M, B for thousands, millions, billions)

**Dates:**
- Use consistent format throughout (Month Year or MM/YYYY)
- Current date in cover letter as [Date]

## CONDITIONAL GENERATION LOGIC

### Match Score-Based Inclusion

**Score 85-100 (Excellent Match):**
- Generate ALL 50 sections
- Maximum depth and detail for all sections
- Include all frameworks, templates, and examples

**Score 70-84 (Strong Match):**
- Generate Sections 1-25
- Omit advanced frameworks (Sections 26-40, 44-50)
- Focus on core prep and application materials

**Score 50-69 (Moderate Match):**
- Generate Sections 1-15
- Core prep only (job overview, match analysis, STAR stories, 30-60-90, application materials)
- Skip intermediate and advanced sections

**Score <49 (Weak Match):**
- Store in database but do not generate guide
- Create minimal record for tracking purposes only

### Data Availability-Based Omissions

**If product data unavailable:**
- Skip or simplify Section 4 (Product Overview)
- Use generic "the company's platform" references

**If hiring manager unknown:**
- Skip Section 6 (Hiring Manager Profile)
- Or create generic "hiring manager" references without specifics

**If no Glassdoor data:**
- Skip Section 13 (Culture Intel)
- Or use LinkedIn employee sentiment as alternative

**If no network connections:**
- Simplify Section 17 (Network Leverage)
- Focus on alternative application strategies

**Data Accuracy:**

- [ ] Job requirements match actual posting

- [ ] All required sections present (based on match score)
- [ ] Section numbering is sequential

**ATS Optimization (Resume/Cover Letter):**

## ERROR HANDLING

### Common Generation Errors & Fixes

**Error: Variable Not Found**
- Log the missing variable
- Use fallback data source (next in priority order)
- If no fallback available, use "[Research Needed]" or omit
- Never output {{VARIABLE}} in final guide

**Error: Insufficient Data for Section**
- If Section 1-10: Mark section as incomplete, use placeholder
- If Section 11+: Skip section entirely
- Document omission in generation log

**Error: Conflicting Data Sources**
- Prioritize most recent/authoritative source
- If job posting vs enrichment conflict: use job posting
- If enrichment vs user profile conflict: use enrichment for job-specific, user profile for background

**Error: Generated Content Too Short**
- Re-run generation with explicit length requirement
- Add context and examples to reach minimum word count
- Never pad with filler—add substantive content only

**Error: ATS Keyword Density Too Low**
- Identify missing keywords from job posting
- Organically incorporate into relevant sections
- Prioritize Section 2, 41, 42, 43 for keyword placement

## EXAMPLES OF EXCELLENT vs. POOR GENERATION

### Example 1: Match Analysis Section

**❌ POOR (Too Generic, No Specifics):**
```
The user has extensive experience in customer success and would be a great fit for this role. They have worked with enterprise clients and understand how to build strong relationships.
```

**✅ EXCELLENT (Specific, Quantified, Detailed):**
```
With 14+ years in B2B SaaS and enterprise consultative sales/customer success, the user brings proven experience defining, executing, and iterating client success programs for Enterprise and Mid-Market clients. They regularly manage multi-threaded portfolios spanning C-suite, VP, Director, and operational teams, having built repeatable "trusted advisor" relationships across 50+ enterprise accounts worth $15M+ in ARR. Their track record includes moving 20-30% of at-risk accounts from amber/red to green health status through focused adoption initiatives and executive alignment, while maintaining 95%+ retention rates.
```

### Example 2: STAR Story

**❌ POOR (Vague, No Numbers, Generic):**
```
**Situation:** A customer was unhappy and considering leaving.
**Task:** I needed to save the account.
**Action:** I worked with the customer to understand their issues and implemented solutions.
**Result:** The customer stayed and was happy.
```

**✅ EXCELLENT (Specific, Detailed, Quantified):**
```
**Situation:** Enterprise healthcare client ($850K ARR) signaled intent to not renew due to <40% platform adoption and mounting frustration with integration complexity 90 days before renewal.

**Task:** Secure renewal, improve adoption to >70%, and position for $200K expansion within 180 days.

**Action:** 
- Conducted emergency stakeholder summit with CFO, CIO, and VP Operations to rebuild trust
- Deployed intensive 45-day adoption acceleration program with dedicated implementation engineer
- Created custom integration bridge to legacy EHR system (technical blocker for 6 months)
- Established weekly executive steering committee with clear KPIs and milestone tracking
- Delivered three targeted training sessions for clinical and administrative users

**Result:** Secured 3-year renewal ($2.55M total contract value), achieved 78% platform adoption within 60 days, and expanded into two additional business units ($380K expansion) within 5 months. Client became reference account and contributed to case study featured at industry conference.
```

### Example 3: Cover Letter Opening

**❌ POOR (Stiff, Generic, No Hook):**
```
I am writing to formally apply for the Customer Success Executive position at your company. I have many years of experience in customer success and believe I would be a good candidate for this role.
```

**✅ EXCELLENT (Natural, Specific, Confident):**
```
I'm writing to express my strong interest in the Customer Success Executive position supporting Corrigo's enterprise and mid-market clients. With over 14 years driving customer success, retention, and expansion across complex SaaS platforms, I'm confident I can deliver immediate impact to JLL's facilities management technology division.
```

## FINAL GENERATION COMMAND

   - Query jobs database for job_id

2. **Determine Scope:**

   - Determine sections to include (1-50, 1-25, or 1-15)

3. **Generate Content:**
   - Populate all {{VARIABLES}} following priority order
   - Apply section-specific generation standards
   - Ensure minimum word counts met
   - Incorporate job-specific keywords

4. **Quality Check:**
   - Run through QA checklist
   - Verify no {{VARIABLES}} remain unpopulated
   - Check tone, specificity, quantification
   - Validate ATS optimization

5. **Format & Export:**
   - Apply consistent markdown formatting
   - Generate metadata section
   - Save to database (strategy_kits.data as JSON)
   - Export to markdown if requested

6. **Log Generation:**
   - Record: job_id, user_id, generation_date, sections_included, data_sources_used, any_errors
